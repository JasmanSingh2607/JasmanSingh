01 / The Agency

A civilian agency built for discovery.

NASA was established in 1958 to lead the United States' civilian aeronautics and space activities.

From Earth-bound research to deep-space exploration, NASA turns difficult questions into scientific missions.

President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed the National Aeronautics and Space Act on July 29, 1958. NASA officially opened for business on October 1, 1958.

The new organization incorporated the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA), along with space-related programs and facilities transferred from other U.S. government organizations.

The founding legislation directed NASA toward research in aeronautics and space and emphasized peaceful purposes, international cooperation, scientific discovery, and the advancement of aeronautical and space technology.
